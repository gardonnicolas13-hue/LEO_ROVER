\section{Square-root filtering on the LEO rover: theory, a logic defect, and a three-estimator bench}
\label{sec:sqrtvinsint}

This section continues Sec.~\ref{sec:sqrtvins}, which recorded the discovery
of the defects on the Raspberry Pi build. What follows is the resolution work:
the mathematics that motivates the filter, the two source-level corrections,
the optical defect that neither correction addressed, and the state of the
bench at the time of writing. The two sections are sequential, not
contradictory --- where they disagree numerically, the figures here supersede,
and the divergence is dated.

\subsection{Why a square-root form at all}
\label{ssec:sqrtwhy}

A standard EKF propagates the covariance $\mathbf{P}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$
directly. Two properties make this fragile on an embedded platform. First,
$\mathbf{P}$ must remain symmetric positive semi-definite (PSD), but nothing in
the update equations enforces it: round-off alone can produce a negative
eigenvalue, after which the filter is no longer a filter. Second, its condition
number
%
\begin{equation}
  \kappa(\mathbf{P}) \;=\; \frac{\lambda_{\max}(\mathbf{P})}{\lambda_{\min}(\mathbf{P})}
  \label{eq:cond}
\end{equation}
%
grows as the estimate becomes sharp in some directions and loose in others ---
precisely what happens in VINS, where scale and yaw are weakly observable while
the camera constrains the rest tightly.

The square-root filter (SRF) stores instead an upper-triangular factor
$\mathbf{U}$ with
%
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{U} \;=\; \mathbf{P}.
  \label{eq:chol}
\end{equation}
%
Two consequences follow immediately, and they are the whole argument.

\paragraph{Positive semi-definiteness becomes structural, not hoped for.}
For any $\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$,
%
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{x}^{\top}\mathbf{P}\,\mathbf{x}
  \;=\; \mathbf{x}^{\top}\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{U}\mathbf{x}
  \;=\; \lVert \mathbf{U}\mathbf{x} \rVert_{2}^{2} \;\geq\; 0 .
  \label{eq:psd}
\end{equation}
%
The quadratic form is a squared norm. \emph{Whatever} numerical damage
$\mathbf{U}$ suffers, the $\mathbf{P}$ it represents cannot be indefinite. The
EKF has no equivalent guarantee: it must produce a PSD matrix through
arithmetic that does not enforce it.

\paragraph{The conditioning is halved, in the exact sense.}
Since the singular values of $\mathbf{U}$ are the square roots of the
eigenvalues of $\mathbf{P}$,
%
\begin{equation}
  \kappa(\mathbf{U}) \;=\; \sqrt{\kappa(\mathbf{P})} .
  \label{eq:kappa}
\end{equation}
%
In floating point with $t$ mantissa bits, a linear solve on a matrix of
condition number $\kappa$ loses on the order of $\log_{2}\kappa$ significant
bits. Writing $t_{\mathrm{eff}}$ for the bits that survive,
%
\begin{equation}
  t_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{EKF}} \approx t - \log_{2}\kappa(\mathbf{P}),
  \qquad
  t_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{SRF}} \approx t - \log_{2}\kappa(\mathbf{U})
                                      = t - \tfrac{1}{2}\log_{2}\kappa(\mathbf{P}).
  \label{eq:bits}
\end{equation}
%
The SRF loses \emph{half} as many bits, which is the same as saying it behaves
as though its mantissa were twice as long. Concretely, with the
$\lambda_{\min}=3.28\times10^{-9}$ measured in Table~\ref{tab:pdcheck} and a
$\lambda_{\max}$ of order $10^{-2}$, $\kappa(\mathbf{P})\sim3\times10^{6}$:
a covariance-form filter would surrender $\log_{2}(3\times10^{6})\approx21$ of
the $24$ mantissa bits of an IEEE-754 \texttt{float}, leaving three. The
square-root form surrenders about eleven and keeps thirteen. This is the
quantitative content of the upstream claim that $\sqrt{\text{VINS}}$ runs in
32-bit single precision, and it is why the initial diagnosis of this project
--- that the divergence was a precision problem --- was a reasonable
hypothesis before it was refuted (Sec.~\ref{ssec:retract}).

\paragraph{Measured on this robot.} Table~\ref{tab:pdcheck} reports the minimum
eigenvalue of the published $6\times6$ pose covariance, sampled over a 25\,s
window (2\,105 messages), all three estimators fed \emph{bit-identical} inputs.

The square-root filter operates three to five orders of magnitude closer to
singularity than the other two and \emph{still} never loses definiteness. That
is Eq.~\eqref{eq:psd} observed rather than asserted.

\subsubsection{Propagation without ever forming \texorpdfstring{$\mathbf{P}$}{P}}

The factor is propagated without the covariance ever being assembled. Given the
state transition $\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{k-1}$ and process noise
$\mathbf{W}_{k-1}=\mathbf{W}_{k-1}^{\top/2}\mathbf{W}_{k-1}^{1/2}$, the
covariance recursion
$\mathbf{P}_{k|k-1}=\boldsymbol{\Phi}\mathbf{P}_{k-1|k-1}\boldsymbol{\Phi}^{\top}+\mathbf{W}$
is realised by stacking and triangularising:
%
\begin{equation}
  \begin{bmatrix}
    \mathbf{W}_{k-1}^{1/2} \\[2pt]
    \mathbf{U}_{k-1|k-1}\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{k-1}^{\top}
  \end{bmatrix}
  \;\stackrel{\mathrm{QR}}{=}\;
  \mathbf{Q}_{k-1}
  \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{U}_{k|k-1} \\ \mathbf{0} \end{bmatrix}.
  \label{eq:qrprop}
\end{equation}
%
Because $\mathbf{Q}$ is orthonormal, $\mathbf{Q}^{\top}\mathbf{Q}=\mathbf{I}$
and the product of the stack with itself is preserved exactly:
$\mathbf{U}_{k|k-1}^{\top}\mathbf{U}_{k|k-1}
=\boldsymbol{\Phi}\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{U}\boldsymbol{\Phi}^{\top}+\mathbf{W}
=\mathbf{P}_{k|k-1}$. The covariance is never squared and never inverted, which
is where an EKF spends most of its conditioning budget.

\emph{This is also the recursion whose noise block was defective in this
package} --- see Sec.~\ref{sssec:rank3}: the $\mathbf{W}^{1/2}$ of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:qrprop} was assembled rank-deficient, and because QR preserves
$\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{U}$ exactly, it preserved the defect exactly too.

\subsection{The ZUPT update, and a logic defect that made it unusable}
\label{ssec:zupt}

\subsubsection{What a zero-velocity update does}

At a genuine standstill the true body velocity is zero, which is a measurement
free of charge. With state error $\tilde{\mathbf{x}}$ and measurement Jacobian
$\mathbf{H}$, the residual is simply
%
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{r} \;=\; \mathbf{0} - {}^{G}\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{I}
  \;=\; -\,\mathbf{H}\tilde{\mathbf{x}} + \mathbf{n},
  \qquad \mathbf{n}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{R}).
  \label{eq:zuptres}
\end{equation}
%
Whether this residual is \emph{consistent} with the current state and its
uncertainty is decided by the Mahalanobis distance
%
\begin{equation}
  \chi^{2} \;=\; \mathbf{r}^{\top}\,
  \bigl(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{P}\mathbf{H}^{\top} + \mathbf{R}\bigr)^{-1}\mathbf{r},
  \label{eq:zuptchi2}
\end{equation}
%
accepted when $\chi^{2} < \gamma\,\chi^{2}_{0.95}(\dim\mathbf{r})$.

\subsubsection{The defect}

The package gates the \emph{rejection} as follows
(\texttt{UpdaterZeroVelocity.cpp}, as cloned):

\begin{verbatim}
// We need to pass the chi2 and not be above our velocity threshold
if (!disparity_passed && (chi2 > options_.chi2_multipler * chi2_check ||
                          state->imu->vel().norm() > zupt_max_velocity_)) {
  ... reject ...
}
\end{verbatim}

The leading \verb|!disparity_passed &&| governs the \emph{entire} rejection.
Once the image-disparity test passes, neither $\chi^{2}$ nor the velocity bound
is ever evaluated. The comment one line above states the intended semantics
--- \emph{``We need to pass the chi2''} --- and the code does the opposite.

Measured on this rover with the gate fully enabled
(\texttt{zupt\_chi2\_multipler:~1}, threshold correctly computed at $65.171$),
robot stationary: \textbf{290 updates accepted, 0 rejected}, with
$\chi^{2}=1.34\times10^{57}$ and $\lVert\mathbf{v}\rVert=1.0\times10^{23}$\,m/s.
The thresholds were correct; they were simply never consulted. Position
diverged to $2.06\times10^{15}$\,m.

\subsubsection{The correction, and why the two guards are not symmetric}

The two tests answer different questions, and only one of them may legitimately
be overridden.

\begin{itemize}
  \item The \textbf{velocity bound} reads the filter's \emph{own} estimate
        ${}^{G}\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{I}$. It is therefore circular: once the filter
        diverges, that estimate is wrong, and the bound would block the only
        mechanism able to recover it. Breaking that circularity with an
        independent witness --- image disparity, which does not depend on the
        state --- is exactly what the disparity test is for. \emph{This
        override is legitimate and was kept.}
  \item The \textbf{$\chi^{2}$ test} of Eq.~\eqref{eq:zuptchi2} asks whether the
        measurement is consistent with the state and its covariance. A value of
        $10^{57}$ says the update is nonsense; applying it injects that
        nonsense into the state. No external witness can make an inconsistent
        update acceptable. \emph{This override must not exist.}
\end{itemize}

\begin{verbatim}
const bool chi2_over = (options_.chi2_multipler > 0) &&
                       (chi2 > options_.chi2_multipler * chi2_check);
const bool vel_over  = state->imu->vel().norm() > zupt_max_velocity_;

if (chi2_over || (!disparity_passed && vel_over)) { ... reject ... }
\end{verbatim}

The documented \texttt{chi2\_multipler:~0} (``disparity only'') semantics are
preserved: with $0$ the \emph{threshold} is not applied. A non-finite
$\chi^{2}$ is rejected in every mode, since \texttt{nan} does not mean
``excellent measurement'', it means ``$\chi^{2}$ not computable''.

\subsubsection{Why the disparity override collapses at kinematic extremes}
\label{sssec:plafond}

The corrected condition is still a disjunction in which one term remains
gated. Writing $C$ for ``$\chi^{2}$ exceeds threshold'', $V$ for
``$\lVert\mathbf{v}\rVert$ exceeds \texttt{zupt\_max\_velocity}'' and $D$
for ``disparity passed'', the reject predicate is
%
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{R} \;=\; C \;\vee\; (\lnot D \wedge V),
  \label{eq:reject}
\end{equation}
%
against the original $\mathcal{R}_{0} = \lnot D \wedge (C \vee V)$. The
correction moved $C$ out of the gate, which was necessary and sufficient for the
$\chi^{2}$ pathology. It deliberately left $V$ inside, on the argument that
$V$ is \emph{circular}: it reads
${}^{G}\hat{\mathbf{v}}_{I}$, the filter's own estimate, so a diverged filter
reports a false $V$ and would veto the only mechanism able to recover it. The
disparity test breaks that circularity with a witness computed from images
alone, independent of the state.

That argument is sound on a bounded domain and fails outside it. Let
$v_{\text{phys}}$ be the platform's physical maximum. For
$\lVert\mathbf{v}\rVert \lesssim v_{\text{phys}}$, a true $V$ is genuinely
ambiguous --- the filter may be right or wrong --- and deferring to an
independent witness is the correct arbitration. For
$\lVert\mathbf{v}\rVert \ggg v_{\text{phys}}$ there is no ambiguity left to
arbitrate: the proposition ``this rover travels at
$922\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$'' has prior probability zero, and no image-domain
evidence can raise it. Deferring to $D$ there is not arbitration, it is
abdication.

The measurement that forced the point: filter at
$\lVert\mathbf{v}\rVert=922.79\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$,
\texttt{zupt\_max\_velocity} set to $1.5$, and \textbf{zero rejections
logged}. $D$ held --- the scene looked static --- so $V$ was never consulted.
That is $3500\times$ the rover's measured maximum of
$0.264\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ (Table~\ref{tab:vmax}).

The remedy is a term outside the lattice: an unconditional predicate evaluated
before Eq.~\eqref{eq:reject},
%
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{A} \;=\; \lnot\,\mathrm{finite}\bigl(\lVert\mathbf{v}\rVert\bigr)
             \;\vee\; \lVert\mathbf{v}\rVert > v_{\max}^{\mathrm{abs}},
  \qquad v_{\max}^{\mathrm{abs}} = 3.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}},
  \label{eq:absolu}
\end{equation}
%
with $\mathcal{R}' = \mathcal{A} \vee \mathcal{R}$. The finiteness clause is
not decoration: \texttt{nan} fails every ordinary comparison, so
$\texttt{nan} > x$ evaluates false and a \texttt{nan} velocity would slip
through a bare inequality --- \texttt{nan} does not mean ``small'', it means
``not computable''.

$v_{\max}^{\mathrm{abs}}$ is a compile-time constant, not a YAML field, and

faster platform should cost a code change and a rebuild, which leaves a trace
in version control.

\paragraph{What this bound does not do.} It does not make a diverged filter
converge. ZUPT is precisely the mechanism that pulls
$\lVert\mathbf{v}\rVert$ back toward zero; refusing it cannot help recovery,
and in isolation makes it marginally worse. Its value is different and real:
it prevents an update computed from a corrupted covariance from injecting
further error into the state, and --- the reason that actually justifies the
code --- it converts a silent failure into a logged one. The diagnosis
reported here cost hours precisely because the filter diverged while
\emph{accepting} every update without a word.

\subsubsection{A second, independent defect: a rank-deficient noise block}
\label{sssec:rank3}

\texttt{StateHelper::propagate\_zero\_motion()} augments the square-root
covariance with the bias random-walk noise. It allocates \emph{six} noise rows
and then writes both $3\times3$ blocks to \emph{the same} rows $0$--$2$, leaving
rows $3$--$5$ zero. Writing $\mathbf{W}^{1/2}$ for that block and using the fact
that $\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{W}^{1/2\top}\mathbf{W}^{1/2}$ is invariant under the QR
that follows, the implied noise covariance becomes
%
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{W}_{\text{bug}} =
  \begin{bmatrix}
    \sigma_{wb}^{2}\Delta t\,\mathbf{I}_{3} & \sigma_{wb}\sigma_{ab}\Delta t\,\mathbf{I}_{3}\\[2pt]
    \sigma_{wb}\sigma_{ab}\Delta t\,\mathbf{I}_{3} & \sigma_{ab}^{2}\Delta t\,\mathbf{I}_{3}
  \end{bmatrix},
  \qquad
  \operatorname{rank}\mathbf{W}_{\text{bug}} = 3 ,
  \label{eq:wbug}
\end{equation}
%
instead of the correct block-diagonal, full-rank
$\mathbf{W}=\Delta t\,\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_{wb}^{2}\mathbf{I}_{3},
\sigma_{ab}^{2}\mathbf{I}_{3})$.

Two things make this defect hard to see. The diagonal --- the per-axis
variances --- is \emph{unchanged}, so nothing looks wrong on inspection. And
the spurious off-diagonal asserts a correlation
$\rho = \sigma_{wb}\sigma_{ab}/(\sigma_{wb}\sigma_{ab}) $ of exactly one
between the gyroscope and accelerometer bias random walks, which are physically
independent. Numerically, with the deployed $\sigma$ values, each update
injects $\rho \approx 0.044$.

The consequence matches the observation exactly. At rest, ZUPT constrains
angular rate directly, so $\mathbf{b}_{g}$ is well observed and corrected; the
spurious coupling pours every such correction into $\mathbf{b}_{a}$, which is
nearly unobservable at rest and which nothing pulls back. Measured before
correction: $\mathbf{b}_{a} = (-1.47\times10^{5},\,1.11\times10^{4},\,
3.69\times10^{4})$\,m/s$^{2}$ --- seven orders of magnitude outside anything
physical --- while $\mathbf{b}_{g}=(-0.0023,\,0.0009,\,-0.0080)$\,rad/s
remained perfectly sane and the attitude quaternion stayed stable.

The same augmentation is performed \emph{correctly} some one hundred lines away
in \texttt{UpdaterZeroVelocity.cpp}, where the analogous matrix is built
block-diagonal and full-rank. Two implementations of one computation had
diverged. The fix aligns the second on the first.

\paragraph{Honesty note.} This correction was applied \emph{first} and did not,
on its own, stop the divergence. Whether it is necessary \emph{in addition to}
the guard correction has not been isolated; only that it is independently wrong
and that the correct form already existed in the same package. Both are in
place in every measurement reported here.

\subsection{Retracting the single-precision hypothesis}
\label{ssec:retract}

The initial hypothesis attributed the divergence to the upstream default
\texttt{USE\_FLOAT=ON}, which types every vector and matrix --- including the
square-root factor itself --- as \texttt{float}. The package was rebuilt in
double precision (\texttt{-DUSE\_FLOAT=OFF}, no source modification, the CMake
\texttt{option()} accepting a command-line value) and \textbf{the defect
reproduced identically}. The hypothesis is therefore refuted: the failure was
one of logic, not of arithmetic. Double precision did contribute one thing ---
it turned unreadable numerical garbage ($\chi^{2}=\texttt{inf}$) into a legible
state that named the corrupted vector.

\label{ssec:flou}

Both source-level corrections were real, and neither addressed the failure that
actually ends a drive. On the dynamic run of 2026-08-21 the two pure
visual-inertial filters lost their entire visual channel within two seconds of
the wheels turning, and only then fabricated velocity.

\subsubsection{The three suspects, and how two were eliminated}

\paragraph{Network --- eliminated by measurement.} \texttt{tools/audit\_flux\_images.py},
45\,s, sensor timestamps (never arrival times, which would fold WiFi jitter
into the sensor's):

\begin{verbatim}
infra1   675 images / 45.0 s -> 15.01 Hz (nominal 15)
         dt median 66.7 ms | jitter 0.0 ms (0.0 %) | min 66.7 max 66.7
         GAPS: 0 -> 0 frames lost | reversals 0 | duplicates 0
infra2   identical
STEREO   cam0<->cam1 offset: median 0.00 ms | max 0.00 ms
\end{verbatim}

Zero jitter, zero loss, zero reordering, exact stereo synchrony. The image
path is unrelated to the $40\,\%$ IMU jitter measured separately: they are
different transports.

\paragraph{KLT parameters --- eliminated by arithmetic.} The tracker's reach is
set by \texttt{win\_size}$=15$ and \texttt{pyr\_levels}$=5$ (compiled in, not
configurable), giving a search radius of
$(\texttt{win\_size}/2)\cdot 2^{\texttt{pyr\_levels}} = 240$\,px. The
inter-frame image displacement induced by body rotation is
%
\begin{equation}
  \Delta u \;=\; \frac{\omega\,f}{f_{\mathrm{cam}}},
  \label{eq:interframe}
\end{equation}
%
with $f=336.37$\,px the focal length and $f_{\mathrm{cam}}=15$\,Hz the frame
rate. At the measured peak $\omega=0.631\,\mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$ this is
$14.1$\,px --- a factor $17$ inside the tracker's reach. Likewise
\texttt{track\_frequency}$=31$\,Hz exceeds the $15.01$\,Hz publication rate, so
every frame is processed and the setting is not a limiter.

\subsubsection{Motion blur: the surviving suspect, modelled}

A point feature imaged during an exposure of duration $t_{\mathrm{exp}}$ while
the body rotates at $\omega$ is smeared across
%
\begin{equation}
  b \;=\; \omega \, t_{\mathrm{exp}} \, f
  \label{eq:blur}
\end{equation}
%
pixels. Equation~\eqref{eq:blur} is the small-angle limit of the exact
projection and is accurate to better than $1\,\%$ for
$\omega t_{\mathrm{exp}} \ll 1$, amply satisfied here.

The measured configuration made $t_{\mathrm{exp}}$ large for a reason that is
documented elsewhere in this report: the IR projector is switched off
(\texttt{laser\_power}$=0$) because its dot pattern polluted the calibration
checkerboard and the beacon LEDs. Without the projector the infrared scene is
dim, and auto-exposure compensates the only way it can --- by opening the
shutter. The camera reported $t_{\mathrm{exp}}=33\,000\,\mu\mathrm{s}$: the
shutter stands open for $33$\,ms of a $66.7$\,ms frame period, half the time.

This ROS wrapper exposes no auto-exposure \emph{limit}: the available controls
are \texttt{enable\_auto\_exposure}, \texttt{exposure} and \texttt{gain}.
Capping is therefore impossible; the exposure must be fixed manually, which
also fixes the gain. Applied: $t_{\mathrm{exp}}=8\,000\,\mu\mathrm{s}$ with
\texttt{gain} raised $16\rightarrow64$ to compensate the $4.1\times$ loss of
light.

rover crossing from a lit area into a dark one has nothing left to compensate:
the image will clip or black out. In a laboratory at constant illumination
this is immaterial; outdoors, or between rooms, it must be re-validated. This
is a real regression in robustness accepted in exchange for a real gain in
feature yield, not a free improvement.

\subsection{Instrumenting the update: where features are actually lost}
\label{ssec:etages}

Every figure in the two subsections that follow rests on a measurement that
the package could not produce as shipped. \texttt{UpdaterMSCKF.cpp} contains
\emph{no} diagnostic output --- no \texttt{PRINT\_ALL}, no
\texttt{PRINT\_DEBUG} --- and its single feature counter is commented out.
Running the node at \texttt{verbosity: ALL} therefore reveals nothing about
this stage, which is why an earlier attempt to instrument it returned an empty
capture.

Five counters were added, one at each \texttt{erase} of the pipeline, in the
order they execute: features removed for having fewer than two usable
measurements; triangulation failure; refinement failure; Jacobian failure;
reprojection failure; and $\chi^{2}$ rejection. A single line is emitted per
update:

\begin{verbatim}
[MSCKF-ETAGES]: entree N | <2mesures N | avant_tri N | tri N | refine N
                | jacob N | reproj N | chi2 N | SORTIE N
\end{verbatim}

\paragraph{A metric that had to be discarded.} This instrumentation also
invalidated the measure used earlier in the investigation. The
\texttt{/points\_msckf} point cloud was read as ``features consumed per
update'', but it is populated from \texttt{good\_features\_MSCKF}, which is
cleared only inside \texttt{do\_feature\_propagate\_update}. When a
zero-velocity update pre-empts the camera path --- which it does on nearly
every frame at rest --- that function is never reached, the vector retains its
previous contents, and the visualiser republishes them indefinitely. A capture
showing $1\,352$ messages averaging $78$ features was recorded while
\texttt{UpdaterMSCKF::update()} was called \emph{zero} times. Any conclusion
drawn from \texttt{/points\_msckf} alone is therefore unsafe, and the
per-stage counters are used instead throughout what follows.

\subsection{The geometric ceiling: base-to-depth ratio and the limits of pure
visual-inertial odometry on a slow platform}
\label{ssec:parallaxe}

The preceding sections localised, corrected and measured a series of software
defects. This one addresses a different class of limit --- one that no
parameter can lift, because it is set by the vehicle's geometry and speed
rather than by its code. It matters for the conclusions of this report: it
explains why the wheel-aided estimator survives drives that both pure
visual-inertial filters fail, and it bounds what any future tuning effort can
be expected to achieve.

essentially frozen at $9\,\mathrm{cm}$.

\subsubsection{Why depth collapses at room scale}

For a rectified stereo pair, depth follows from disparity $d$ as $Z = bf/d$.
Differentiating and substituting back gives the standard first-order
propagation of disparity error into depth error:
%
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_Z \;=\; \frac{Z^{2}}{b\,f}\,\sigma_d .
  \label{eq:sigmaz}
\end{equation}
%
The quadratic dependence on $Z$ is the whole difficulty: uncertainty does not
degrade gracefully with distance, it degrades as the square. With
$\sigma_d = 1\,\mathrm{px}$ --- optimistic, given the $\pm 0.19\,\mathrm{px}$
calibration residual and the residual motion blur quantified in
Sec.~\ref{ssec:flou} --- Eq.~\eqref{eq:sigmaz} gives Table~\ref{tab:sigmaz}.

\begin{tabular}{rrrrr}
\hline
$Z$ (m) & $b/Z$ & $\sigma_Z$ stereo (m) & $\sigma_Z$ motion-only (m) & rel.\ error (stereo) \\

$3$  & $1{:}33$  & $0.297$ & $1.520$  & $10\,\%$ \\

At the distances that dominate an indoor scene --- walls, furniture and
doorways at three to five metres --- the base-to-depth ratio falls between
$1{:}33$ and $1{:}56$, and the depth of a feature is known only to $30$ to
$83\,\mathrm{cm}$. A feature triangulated with that uncertainty, then
reprojected into the other poses of a $25$-clone sliding window, can plausibly
land behind the image plane of one of them.

This is precisely the failure the instrumentation of Sec.~\ref{ssec:etages}
counts. The rejection is issued by \texttt{UpdaterHelper.cpp} on the condition
$Z_{\mathrm{cam}} < 0.1\,\mathrm{m}$, reported as \emph{``Negative depth
detected''}, and it accounted for $35.8\,\%$ of all features entering the
update before any correction. It is not a threshold that is set too tight: a
feature genuinely cannot lie behind the camera that observes it. It is the
downstream symptom of a depth estimate whose uncertainty is comparable to the
depth itself.

\subsubsection{What wheel odometry contributes, and why it is decisive here}

MINS consumes \texttt{/joint\_states\_mins} in addition to the stereo pair and
the IMU; the two pure visual-inertial filters do not. Under the conditions of
Table~\ref{tab:sigmaz} this is not a marginal advantage but the difference
between an observable system and a poorly observable one, for three distinct
reasons.

\paragraph{Metric scale becomes directly observed.} In a visual-inertial
system, scale is recovered by integrating accelerometer measurements over the
interval during which a feature is tracked, and is therefore only as good as
the bias estimate over that interval. Wheel encoders measure displacement
directly, in metres, with an error that grows linearly with distance rather
than quadratically with time. The estimator no longer needs to extract scale
from a geometry that, per Table~\ref{tab:sigmaz}, cannot supply it reliably.

\paragraph{Velocity is constrained without vision.} When features collapse ---
whether from a texture-poor scene, motion blur, or the triangulation regime
described above --- a pure VIO filter has no measurement left and falls back
to open-loop inertial integration, in which an accelerometer bias error
$\varepsilon$ produces a position error $\tfrac{1}{2}\varepsilon t^{2}$. The
divergences recorded in Sec.~\ref{ssec:etat} follow this signature. A wheel
measurement bounds velocity at every step and removes the double integration
entirely.

\paragraph{The planar constraint is exploited rather than ignored.} MINS uses
a \texttt{Wheel2DAng} model, which asserts planar motion. This is
simultaneously its strength on $x$ and $y$ and its documented weakness on
$z$: the Jacobian $\partial h/\partial z$ is identically zero, so the vertical
axis receives no wheel information whatsoever and is left to the camera alone.
The measurements of this campaign show both faces of that trade-off --- MINS
holding $x$ and $y$ to within centimetres over drives where both VIO filters
diverged, while its own $z$ drifted to several metres and, in one recorded
instance, to $1707\,\mathrm{m}$.

\paragraph{Consequence for any comparison in this report.} A drive in which
MINS reports $3.83\,\mathrm{m}$ and $\sqrt{\text{VINS}}$ reports
$431\,\mathrm{m}$ over the same sixteen seconds is \emph{not} a measurement of
square-root versus covariance arithmetic. It is a measurement of what wheel
odometry is worth when the visual channel cannot carry the estimate on its
own. Every three-way figure in this report must be read with that asymmetry
stated.

\subsubsection{The refinement trade-off: fewer bad depths, or fewer depths}

\texttt{fi\_refine\_features} controls whether the linear triangulation is
followed by a Levenberg--Marquardt refinement of the feature position. The
deployed $\sqrt{\text{VINS}}$ configuration had it disabled --- alone among
the three estimators, openVINS and MINS both leaving it at the upstream
default of \texttt{true}. Since low parallax is exactly the regime in which a
linear solution is least trustworthy, disabling the non-linear refinement
removes the correction where it is most needed.

Enabling it produced the shift in Table~\ref{tab:refine}, measured with
identical instrumentation and the same geometry.

Two readings follow, and they must be kept separate.

\paragraph{The targeted stage improves, and by the expected mechanism.}
Jacobian rejection falls from $35.8\,\%$ to $29.4\,\%$, an $18\,\%$ relative
reduction, and the underlying \emph{``Negative depth detected''} count drops
from $17\,242$ to $3\,789$ over comparable windows. The refinement is doing
what it is for: a feature whose linear depth estimate was inconsistent with
its observations is moved to a position that reprojects in front of every
camera that saw it.

\paragraph{The aggregate does not improve, because the losses move upstream.}
Triangulation rejection rises from $20.6\,\%$ to $25.7\,\%$, and total yield
is unchanged within the noise of these measurements ($10.9\,\%$ against
$9.2\,\%$). This is the honest interpretation: the refinement does not
manufacture well-conditioned features, it fails the ill-conditioned ones
\emph{earlier and more explicitly}. A feature that previously survived
triangulation only to be discarded later for negative depth is now rejected at
the stage that should have rejected it.

That is a real improvement in the diagnostic quality of the pipeline and a
negligible one in its output. It is worth keeping --- it aligns
$\sqrt{\text{VINS}}$ with the other two estimators and with upstream, and it
moves rejections to where they are interpretable --- but it should not be
presented as a fix for the yield.

\paragraph{The ceiling stands.} Across the three configurations, between
$8.5\,\%$ and $10.9\,\%$ of features entering the update survive it, from an
image in which $165$ FAST corners are demonstrably detectable during motion
(Sec.~\ref{ssec:flou}). Neither the camera--IMU extrinsics
(Sec.~\ref{ssec:archi}) nor the refinement setting moved that figure
materially. The limit identified in Table~\ref{tab:sigmaz} --- a $9\,\mathrm{cm}$
baseline observing a scene at three to five metres, with a vehicle too slow to
accumulate meaningful additional parallax --- is the operative constraint, and
it is a property of the platform rather than of the estimator.

\paragraph{What would lift it.} Three avenues follow directly from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:sigmaz}, in decreasing order of practicality on this rover: a
longer stereo baseline, which enters linearly; operation at shorter range,
which enters quadratically and is by far the most effective; or wheel-aided
fusion, which sidesteps the triangulation problem rather than solving it and
is the approach MINS already demonstrates. Faster driving would help only
marginally: even a fourfold increase in $v_{\max}$ would leave the inter-frame
displacement below the stereo baseline.

\subsection{What is settled, and what is not}
\label{ssec:bilan}

\paragraph{Settled, with measurements.} Four results are reproducible and
carry their numbers: the $\chi^{2}$ override defect and its correction
(Table~\ref{tab:zuptfix}); the rank-deficient bias-noise block,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:wbug}, whose correct form already existed elsewhere in the same
package; the refutation of the single-precision hypothesis
(Sec.~\ref{ssec:retract}); and the exposure cap, Table~\ref{tab:expcap}.
Positive-definiteness under Eq.~\eqref{eq:psd} is observed rather than
assumed, at $\lambda_{\min}=3.28\times10^{-9}$.

\paragraph{Not settled.} Neither pure visual-inertial filter survives a drive
on this platform. The absolute bound of Eq.~\eqref{eq:absolu} intercepts the
runaway and logs it; it does not prevent it, and the report should not be read
as claiming otherwise. The exposure cap is validated \emph{at rest} only ---
$12\rightarrow72$ features --- and the in-motion measurement that would
justify it has not been taken.

\paragraph{A structural caveat that governs every comparison here.} MINS
consumes \texttt{/joint\_states\_mins}; the two visual-inertial filters
consume the stereo pair and the IMU and nothing else. MINS surviving a drive
that the other two fail is therefore not evidence about square-root versus
covariance arithmetic. It is a measurement of what wheel odometry is worth
when the visual channel collapses --- roughly, the difference between
$3.83$\,m and $431$\,m over the same sixteen seconds. Any conclusion drawn
across the three estimators must carry this asymmetry explicitly.

\paragraph{Two hardware conditions invalidated measurements during this
campaign,} and are recorded so that the same trap is not fallen into twice.
The ground PC reached $86\,^{\circ}$C against a manufacturer threshold of
$82\,^{\circ}$C, with the browser consuming more CPU than MINS itself; and the
battery fell to $10.61$\,V, below the $30\,\%$ under which yaw authority is
known to degrade on this chassis, so a commanded rotation is no longer the
rotation performed. \texttt{tools/relance\_estimateurs.sh} now refuses to
start a run when either condition holds. A measurement taken under them
proves nothing, and the discipline of refusing it is cheaper than the hours
spent interpreting one.
